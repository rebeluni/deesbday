Happy Birthday Darp 🤎


Do you remember the day you sat next to me in class — not because you chose to, but because you'd fought with your regular partners? I said "anyway, you'll only be here for today" and you said "no, I'll sit every day." Which, in retrospect, was such a Darp thing to say. A quiet, calm little challenge that you then proceeded to win without even making it a big deal.

That's kind of how our entire friendship has been, actually.

I think about the early days a lot. The two of us on roads we didn't know, literally just walking before and after classes just for the hell of it. No destination, no vehicle, no plan, just walking because we felt like it and being slightly unhinged and completely okay with it. Those stories feel sacred in the way that only the really specific, really small things do.

But here's what I've been thinking about lately — we spend so much time honouring the early stories that we sometimes forget to notice what's happening right now. And right now is also really good. Right now is scooty rides home, taking the scooty out of Golden Nest for the first time only to immediately get caught by traffic police and deciding *that* was prime selfie time, Rajasthani delicacies, and you-plus-Naina equalling the sisters-I-didn't-get-at-home package. Right now is garba, which is — without question — the most I see you in any given year, and somehow always not enough. Right now is two people becoming who they were always going to be, just closer to it than before.

Fellow 3 mulank. Make of that what you will.

You are a wonderful, beautiful, brave woman. And I know you're figuring it out. I can already see us living those lives — the villas, the NGOs, the Airbnb business, the trips that make us real travellers and not just tourists. We are genuinely getting closer to all of it every single day.

I love that you go as deep as I do. That we can talk about feminism and mythology and the absolute state of things and you get as angry as I am about everything that deserves anger. That is incredibly rare and I do not take it for granted.

Also — capitalism is going to end us and we both know it and I love that we hate it together.

I don't say it normally. But you know.

You're loved. 🤎

— Ankita
